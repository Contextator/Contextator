4. Running an instance is your responsibility

If you deploy Contextator, you are the operator, and that carries duties this software cannot perform for you:

  • Access control. The dashboard requires a personal account, and roles decide who reaches which project. ADMIN_TOKEN remains as machine access to the same API and acts with root permissions, so treat it like a root password and keep it out of browsers. The MCP endpoints have a separate door: a project's /mcp/* endpoint is open unless you require a bearer token on it, and while it is open anyone who can reach it reads that project's indexed documents. Deciding which projects to close, and keeping the server on a private network or behind an authenticating proxy, is yours.
  • The tokens you issue. An MCP token is a bearer credential with no identity attached: whoever holds it reads everything indexed in that project. Hand them out over channels you trust, give them names you will recognise later, and revoke the ones you no longer know the whereabouts of.
  • The accounts you create. You decide who gets one, which role they get and when to take it away. Hand temporary passwords over a channel you trust; the software shows each one once and stores only a hash.
  • Secrets. Set a strong SECRET_KEY before storing repository or Notion tokens, and keep it out of version control. Tokens are encrypted with it; lose it and they cannot be decrypted.
  • The content you index. You must hold the rights to index the documents you add, and you must be allowed to expose them to the AI clients you connect. Do not index material you may not copy, and do not use the software to process content unlawfully.
  • Third-party terms. Git hosting providers, Notion and any hosted embedding API you enable have their own terms and rate limits. Your use of them through Contextator is between you and them, under your own credentials.
  • Data protection. If the documents you index contain personal data, the obligations of a controller are yours. See the Privacy Policy.
  • Source, if you modified it. A modified version reachable over a network owes its users the source, under the AGPL. Keep a link to your fork where those users can find it.
